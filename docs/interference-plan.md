# Interference in the session sim, and a model that measures it

## Why

On air the noise is not white. Recordings from 2026-09/10 show:

- intermittent narrowband signals (QRM): a 2.39 kHz signal on 17% of the
  time (20261002-232711), stations coming and going across the passband
  (20260927-005920: the worst 200 Hz band's p90/median 20 dB at p90);
- clustered impulses, ~22 dB over the noise, 1-3 ms, 20-40 ms apart in a
  train (175336, 021310, 214207...).

At a mode's AWGN 10% point, real noise cost 8-24 points of control decodes
against Gaussian noise of the same median (impulses ~8, QRM up to 24); the
blanker recovers ~5 of the impulse loss and none of the QRM's.

The receiver now measures what it can see between bursts (tnc.NoiseProfile:
per sub-band median and p90/median, impulses per minute). The aim: the
outcome model reads that and judges the channel in front of it, not a
blanket caution about wide modes.

## Steps

1. **Interference generator** (`data2g/interference.py`): per receiving
   station, for a whole session, rendered at any time span from a seed.
   Clustered impulse trains; QRM sources (steady/keyed carriers, FSK-like
   hoppers, band-limited noise) at a frequency, bandwidth and
   interference-to-noise ratio (INR, in their own band, over the Gaussian
   floor there), on/off with a duty cycle. Levels are relative to the floor,
   so the cell's SNR does not move them.
2. **Session channel** (`scripts/phy_session.py`): each receiver adds its own
   interference to the bursts it hears, and keeps a NoiseProfile fed with
   the floor plus interference between bursts (not while it transmits), as
   the engine does on air. The profile rides with the burst's measurements.
3. **Model inputs**: the profile in session rows and in
   `predictor.outcome_inputs` (bands relative to the measured burst's band,
   tails, impulse rate, and "no profile yet" as a valid state); the engine
   passes the live profile the same way.
4. **Benchmark and the three arms** below; then on air.

## Judging it

Three models, all retrained on data generated by the same code (v12 can't
be reproduced and predates the receive(head) fix, so it is not an arm):

| arm | training sessions | inputs |
|-----|-------------------|--------|
| A | clean | today's |
| B | with interference | today's |
| C | with interference | today's + noise profile + impulse rate |

- **C vs B** is the question: does measuring help, beyond having seen
  interference in training? **B vs A**: what training on interference alone
  does (expected: caution everywhere).
- **Clean cells** too: C must not lose to A there (no blanket caution).
- **Interference the model hasn't seen**: the generator with new seeds;
  held-out settings (placements, duty cycles, impulse lengths outside the
  training ranges); and **real recorded noise** (noise-only stretches of the
  radio recordings, replayed under the bursts) as the realism test.
- **SNR** against the Gaussian floor (median in-band, peak-referenced as
  now); interference has its own parameters, so arms compare at one point.
- **Paired seeds** (and interference realizations) across arms; results as
  dB shifts of the 10% points and knees per cell, not throughput %.
- **Sim vs air**: before training, the sim's profile values against those
  recorded on air (each rx event carries one since c4cd0a9).

## Results (2026-10-03, rounds 1-4: runs/interference_round*.sh/.out)

| round | data | arm with the noise profile vs A (clean data) |
|-------|------|----------------------------------------------|
| 1 | interfered (v1 draw) only | loses almost everywhere; B (no profile) worse still |
| 2 | clean + v1 | even at high SNR; -15..-25% clean low-SNR fading; carriers +40..+96%, mid +24..+40% |
| 3 | clean + mild + 1 dB floor wander | ~= A on clean (except deepest SNR) and wander cells; collapses past its training range (carriers 359 -> 3 bps) |
| 4 | clean + mild + 1/3 v1, inputs clipped to the training range | no collapse; -7..-13% clean low-SNR fading; mid +47..+78%; carriers/edge mixed (noisy cells) |

- Against its same-data control (no profile) the profile wins large on visible
  narrowband QRM in every round: a real signal.
- Any interfered training data cost clean low-SNR fading 7-25% against
  clean-only training, with or without the profile.
- Out of its training range the profile extrapolates into ruin; clipping
  (predictor.clip_noise) bounds that.
- No learned arm beats A across the board. Next candidate: A's model plus an
  explicit rule on the profile (penalize candidates whose span covers a
  sub-band well above the measured one and often loud).
- The recordings are too few and unrepresentative to fit the sim to; the rec
  cells are weak evidence.
